01 / Data platform Building a Data Platform from ERP to Dashboard +
THE CHALLENGE

One question should have one trusted answer.

Trim-Tex’s operational data lived in Sage X3, and reporting meant ad hoc queries and one-off reports. There was no shared, governed layer, so different teams could get different answers to the same question.

What I built

Incremental ingestion with CDC

Fivetran change tracking replicates X3 (SQL Server) into Snowflake, moving only changed rows with no full reloads and minimal load on the source system.

Source scoping

I mapped the X3 tables across six domains—Sales, Finance, Inventory, Manufacturing, Purchasing, and Customer/Vendor Master—and identified the decode tables needed to translate ERP codes into business meaning.

Layered modeling

dbt Cloud models move data through Bronze, Silver, and Gold layers, orchestrated with Prefect, feeding certified Power BI semantic models.

Cost governance

After metering showed a credit spike from a dev warehouse, I set resource monitors on every active warehouse at dev, prod, and account levels, and removed three dormant warehouses.

RESULT
  • 50+ tables replicated, 150 dbt models, refreshed daily
  • Serves 10 departments and 20 dashboards from one governed layer
  • 20% reduction in Snowflake costs

Stack: Sage X3, Fivetran, Snowflake, dbt Cloud, Prefect, Power BI

02 / Power BI Delivery, governance & adoption +

Consistent by design

Certified shared semantic models support live-connected reports, with no report-layer calculated columns. Built six standardized layout templates from one master template, a Dashboard Dictionary for each dashboard, and rollout communications.

A system people can adopt

Defined a dev-to-prod promotion SOP for a Pro-only environment. Established recurring Office Hours and departmental BI Champions to own adoption. Built the powerbi-dashboard-designer skill to turn a semantic model and discovery notes into a dashboard specification.

WHY IT MATTERS

Reliable models, repeatable delivery, and business ownership make analytics useful beyond the initial dashboard launch.

03 / Enterprise AI Launching an Enterprise AI Program That Found the Real Opportunity +
THE CHALLENGE

Start with the problem. Find where the time goes.

Interest in AI was high, but there was no governance, no way to prioritize, and no clear picture of where the time was actually going. Without that, effort would scatter toward tools instead of problems.

What I built

Launched in three months

Governance framework, acceptable-use and data-handling rules, risk review with IS leadership, and baseline training and lunch-and-learns.

Problem-first intake

I interviewed leaders across departments and scored opportunities by how often the task recurs and how much it costs people in time, manual manipulation, or unfamiliar tools. The starting point was always a process with a problem, never a tool looking for a use.

20 opportunities identified, 2 delivered

The first two are live: self-service AI sales analytics and AR cash application automation. The rest are sequenced by business impact, with a 15-month roadmap behind them.

Revenue-first prioritization

The roadmap favors work that gives time back to the people who sell.

WHAT I LEARNED

Most opportunities weren’t AI problems.

They were non-normal work: the exceptions, lookups, and one-off requests that fall outside a person’s regular routine and eat hours of digging through systems and spreadsheets. Many are fixed with better data models, governed reporting, and conversational access to trusted data. Others need nothing more than a process fix.

That reframed the program. The goal isn’t to deploy AI everywhere. It’s to find the digging, remove it, and let people spend the saved time selling.

RESULT
  • Program launched in 3 months
  • 20 opportunities identified and scored across 10 departments
  • 2 delivered, 3 in progress
  • 2,500 hours saved per year
  • 40 employees trained

Stack: Claude, Snowflake, Power BI, monday.com, M365
